Six things to leave with

  • Interfaces grow roughly with the square of components; integration and test is where complex-system cost hides.
  • Emergent behavior cannot be fully specified in advance; reserve and recurring evaluation are how you fund the unknown.
  • Verification (built right) and validation (built the right thing) are appraisal costs; rework found at integration is internal failure at its most expensive.
  • Agentic systems: cost per task is a distribution with a long tail (retries, tool loops), not a constant.
  • Evaluation harnesses for agents cost more than for single-shot models and must be recurring.

Worked example

Costing one agentic task

  1. Case 3 pilot data: tool calls per message ≈ Poisson(3.4), retries ≈ 11%.
  2. Tokens per task = base + calls × round-trip tokens; cost per task follows the distribution of calls. Plot it; the P90 is what your budget must survive.
  3. Add a 25-second human checkpoint per action. Now compare with the single-shot draft-only assistant. That gap is what 'acting' costs.

Quality management lens

Verification, validation, and integration as quality cost

In complex systems, integration and test is where quality is either built or discovered missing. Verification (built right) and validation (built the right thing) are appraisal costs; rework found at integration is internal failure cost at its most expensive point short of the field.
