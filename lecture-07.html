Simulation-based costing

RAND's cost-growth studies say the typical program overruns its point estimate by 30–50%. Not because estimators are bad — because a point estimate is the most likely outcome, and the distribution has a long right tail. The S-curve is the honest picture.

Objectives

  • Replace point estimates with distributions (triangular, PERT-beta, lognormal) elicited from data or experts and defend the choice.
  • Build a Monte Carlo life-cycle cost model, produce an S-curve, and report the mean, the 50th and 80th percentiles, and the confidence level of the point estimate.
  • Model correlation between cost elements and show its effect on the spread.
  • Update a cost distribution with Bayesian methods as actuals arrive, and produce distribution-free prediction intervals for an ML CER with conformal prediction.

Six things to leave with

  • Uncertainty is what you don't know; risk is what could go wrong. Both belong in the estimate.
  • Replace point inputs with distributions (triangular, PERT, lognormal) and simulate; the output is a distribution of total cost.
  • The S-curve gives the probability of finishing under budget and the reserve needed to fund at the 80th percentile.
  • Correlated inputs widen the spread; independent sampling understates risk.
  • Tornado charts tell you where better data buys the most reduction in spread.
  • Bayesian updating turns year-1 actuals into a sharper forecast; conformal prediction gives distribution-free intervals around a CER.
  • Process capability (Cp, Cpk) is the same idea: how much of the natural variation fits inside the limit.

Segment 1

Why point estimates fail

Cost growth statistics from GAO and RAND; the systematic optimism of single-point estimates; the difference between uncertainty (what we do not know) and risk (what could go wrong).

Segment 2

Building the model

Distribution selection, random sampling, convergence, and the S-curve. Implemented in the lab notebook (numpy); an Excel version without add-ins is possible but optional.

Segment 3

Bayesian updating and conformal intervals

A prior LCC distribution plus observed year-1 actuals yields a posterior; conjugate normal-normal updating by hand, then Markov chain Monte Carlo with PyMC for realistic models. Split conformal prediction gives a calibrated 80% interval around any point estimator, including the gradient-boosted CER from Week 5, without distributional assumptions.

Segment 4

Correlation and sensitivity

Why independent sampling understates risk, rank correlation with the Iman–Conover method, and tornado charts to identify which inputs deserve better data.

Quality management lens

Process capability and simulated cost

Cp and Cpk measure how much of a process's natural variation fits inside specification limits; the Monte Carlo S-curve is the same idea applied to cost against a budget. The probability of exceeding the budget is a capability statement. Statistical process control on cost variance separates noise from signal.

Check yourself

Point estimate sits at the 35th percentile of the S-curve. What does that mean?

A 65% chance of overrunning it; fund at a higher percentile or reduce scope.

Two inputs positively correlated but sampled independently: effect on total-cost spread?

Understated; correlation widens the tails.

Cpk = 0.8 for cost against budget: good or bad?

Bad; a substantial share of outcomes falls outside the limit (Cpk ≥ 1.33 is the usual target).
