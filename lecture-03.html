Advanced analysis of alternatives

Objectives

  • Evaluate alternatives by present worth, annual worth, future worth, internal and external rate of return, and payback, and state when each is misleading.
  • Perform incremental analysis correctly for mutually exclusive alternatives with unequal lives (repeatability versus coterminated assumptions).
  • Identify the multiple-IRR problem and resolve it with the ERR or a PW profile.

Six things to leave with

  • Present worth answers 'how much richer'; rate of return answers 'how efficiently'. Only PW ranks mutually exclusive alternatives correctly.
  • Incremental analysis: order by first cost, then ask whether each increment earns the MARR.
  • Unequal lives need an explicit assumption: repeatability, a coterminated horizon with an imputed value, or capitalized worth.
  • Payback ignores everything after payback; use it as a screen, never as a decision.
  • Non-simple cash flows (mid-life retraining, re-certification) can produce multiple IRRs; use PW or the external rate of return.
  • Inspection, sampling, automated testing, and evaluation suites are mutually exclusive quality alternatives — the same rules apply.

Segment 1

Worth methods versus rate-of-return methods

Why ranking by IRR is wrong for mutually exclusive alternatives, and the incremental procedure $\Delta$IRR that fixes it. Worked example: three options for a document-processing capability (SaaS API, managed cloud model, on-premises fine-tuned model).

Segment 2

Unequal lives

Repeatability assumption, coterminated assumption with an imputed market value, and the capitalized-worth method for long-lived infrastructure. Application: a 3-year GPU refresh cycle against a 5-year licensed-software term.

Segment 3

Rate-of-return pathologies

Non-simple cash flows in AI programs (heavy retraining costs mid-life produce sign changes), Descartes' rule of signs, and the external rate of return.

Worked example

Three ways to inspect a line

  1. A: add an inspector rotation, $0 down, $310k/yr. B: buy vision, $415k down, lower annual, 5-year life. C: subscribe, $30k onboarding, 3-year term.
  2. PW each at 12%. Then the increment B − C: does spending the extra capital earn 12%? The sign pattern tells you whether IRR on the increment is even meaningful.
  3. Change C's renewal price by 15% and see whether the ranking flips. That is the decision-maker's real question.

Quality management lens

Quality alternatives are mutually exclusive alternatives

Manual inspection, statistical sampling, automated test harnesses, and formal evaluation suites are competing ways to buy the same assurance. Incremental analysis applies to them exactly as it does to hardware. Worked example: three appraisal strategies for a deployed classifier.
