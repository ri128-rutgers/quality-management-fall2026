Advanced analysis of alternatives

▶ Interactive lectureOpen notebook in ColabDue Sep 23: Case 1Submit in Canvas

Learning objectives

  • Evaluate alternatives by present worth, annual worth, future worth, internal and external rate of return, and payback, and state when each is misleading.
  • Perform incremental analysis correctly for mutually exclusive alternatives with unequal lives (repeatability versus coterminated assumptions).
  • Identify the multiple-IRR problem and resolve it with the ERR or a PW profile.

Lecture segments

1. Worth methods versus rate-of-return methods

Why ranking by IRR is wrong for mutually exclusive alternatives, and the incremental procedure $\Delta$IRR that fixes it. Worked example: three options for a document-processing capability (SaaS API, managed cloud model, on-premises fine-tuned model).

2. Unequal lives

Repeatability assumption, coterminated assumption with an imputed market value, and the capitalized-worth method for long-lived infrastructure. Application: a 3-year GPU refresh cycle against a 5-year licensed-software term.

3. Rate-of-return pathologies

Non-simple cash flows in AI programs (heavy retraining costs mid-life produce sign changes), Descartes' rule of signs, and the external rate of return.

Quality management lens

Quality alternatives are mutually exclusive alternatives. Manual inspection, statistical sampling, automated test harnesses, and formal evaluation suites are competing ways to buy the same assurance. Incremental analysis applies to them exactly as it does to hardware. Worked example: three appraisal strategies for a deployed classifier.

Agentic AI corner

Have an agent generate the incremental-analysis table for the three inspection alternatives, then audit the ordering of increments. Most agents rank by IRR when asked to compare alternatives; catching that is the point.
