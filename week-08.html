Learning objectives

  • Explain how system-of-systems integration, emergent behavior, and interface count drive cost non-linearly.
  • Apply complexity metrics and integration factors in an estimate.
  • Demonstrate mastery of Weeks 1–7 on the midterm.

Lecture segments

1. Complexity and cost

Interface growth, integration and test as a share of total cost, and why AI components embedded in larger systems raise integration and assurance cost disproportionately.

2. Estimating in the presence of emergence

Reserve-setting for systems whose behavior is not fully specifiable in advance; the role of evaluation harnesses and red-teaming as recurring, not one-time, costs.

Quality management lens

Verification, validation, and integration as quality cost. In complex systems, integration and test is where quality is either built or discovered missing. Verification (built right) and validation (built the right thing) are appraisal costs; rework found at integration is internal failure cost at its most expensive point short of the field.

Agentic AI corner

Costing an agentic system: a single user request may trigger a chain of model calls, tool invocations, and retries. Token cost per task is a distribution with a long tail, not a constant. Evaluation harnesses for agents are more expensive than for single-shot models. This is the complex-systems lecture applied to AI.
