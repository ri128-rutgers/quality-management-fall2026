Objectives

  • Compute straight-line, declining-balance, and MACRS depreciation and construct an after-tax cash flow table.
  • Distinguish capitalized versus expensed technology costs (hardware, purchased software, internally developed software, cloud subscriptions) and explain the cash-flow consequence.
  • Separate real and nominal analysis and apply differential inflation to line items that move differently (compute prices, labor, energy).

Six things to leave with

  • The after-tax cash flow table: before-tax cash flow, depreciation, taxable income, tax, after-tax cash flow. Same table every time.
  • Capitalize or expense changes timing, not total cost — and timing is everything under discounting.
  • MACRS is a schedule, not a forecast of value; models depreciate by drift and competition, not by the tax code.
  • Use item-specific escalation: compute prices fall, energy and labor rise; a single inflation rate hides the decision.
  • Taguchi's loss function: loss grows with the square of deviation from target; 'in spec' is not 'no cost'.
  • For an AI system the loss function prices accuracy shortfall continuously — that is what a service-level decision needs.

Segment 1

After-tax cash flow mechanics

BTCF, depreciation, taxable income, tax, ATCF. The seven-column table you will use for every after-tax problem in the course.

Segment 2

Capitalize or expense

Accelerators and servers are depreciable property; cloud consumption is an operating expense; internally developed software follows capitalization rules that differ between commercial (ASC 350-40) and federal (SFFAS 10) contexts. The classification changes after-tax present worth even when pre-tax cash flows are identical.

Segment 3

Inflation and the cost of compute

Price per unit of compute has historically fallen while energy and skilled labor have risen. Using a single inflation rate hides this. We use actual-dollar analysis with item-specific escalation rates and a market interest rate, then reconcile with a real-dollar analysis.

Worked example

Quality management lens

Taguchi loss and the economics of tolerance

The quadratic loss function replaces the go/no-go view of conformance with a cost that grows with deviation from target. Applied to an ML system, the loss function prices accuracy shortfalls continuously, which is what a service-level decision needs. Also: whether quality-system investments are capitalized or expensed.
