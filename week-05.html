Cost estimating techniques

▶ Interactive lectureOpen notebook in ColabDue Oct 7: Case 2Submit in Canvas

Learning objectives

  • Select among analogy, parametric, engineering build-up, and expert-judgment methods based on program phase and data availability.
  • Fit and apply a cost estimating relationship (CER) with regression, report its statistical quality, and state its valid range.
  • Apply learning-curve theory (unit and cumulative-average) to production and to repeated ML training runs.
  • Build a machine-learned cost estimating relationship (regularized regression, random forest, gradient boosting) with proper cross-validation, compare it against the OLS CER, and explain it with SHAP values.

Lecture segments

1. The estimating methods and when they fit

Early in the life cycle you have analogies; in design you have parameters; in production you have build-ups. Mapping the GAO 12-step process onto a technology program.

2. Parametric estimating and CERs

Ordinary least squares, log-linear forms, and the danger of extrapolating a CER built on CPU-era data to accelerator-era programs. We fit a CER from a public dataset in the lab notebook.

3. Machine learning as an estimating method

When a program database has hundreds of records and dozens of attributes, OLS CERs underfit. Ridge/lasso, random forests, and gradient-boosted trees as CERs; k-fold cross-validation as the honest replacement for in-sample $R^2$; SHAP values to satisfy the reviewer who asks why the model produced that number. We also cover the failure modes GAO reviewers look for: leakage, extrapolation outside the training envelope, and unstable estimates on small datasets.

4. Learning curves

Wright's cumulative-average and Crawford's unit models. Application beyond manufacturing: labeling throughput, MLOps deployment time per model, and the observed decline in cost per training run within a model family.

Quality management lens

Quality of the estimate itself. GAO's four characteristics of a reliable estimate (comprehensive, well-documented, accurate, credible) are a quality standard for cost work. A CER's residual plot is a control chart: patterns in the residuals are assignable causes. Cross-validation is appraisal cost for a model; documentation is prevention cost.

Agentic AI corner

Use an agent to write the cross-validation loop for the CER notebook, then read the code before running it. Agents leak test data into training folds more often than you would expect; that is exactly the appraisal step a quality manager performs on a supplier's process.
