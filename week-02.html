Time value of money and interest theory

▶ Interactive lectureOpen notebook in ColabDue Sep 16: Discussion post and one peer replySubmit in Canvas

Learning objectives

  • Draw cash-flow diagrams and convert between single-payment, uniform-series, gradient, and geometric-series equivalents.
  • Apply nominal, effective, and continuous compounding, and justify the discount rate choice for a public, private, or defense program.
  • Implement the standard factors in a spreadsheet and in Python without relying on interest tables.

Lecture segments

1. Equivalence and the six factors

Money later is worth less than money now; the six factors are just names for the standard conversions between a lump sum, a level series, and a growing series. You will use a provided library rather than derive them. What matters is recognizing the shape of a cost stream: inference volume that grows geometrically while unit prices decline is a geometric gradient, and mis-shaping it is the common error.

2. Effective interest and non-annual periods

Cloud bills arrive monthly; contracts are annual; budgets are fiscal-year. Converting consistently between periods is where most real analyses go wrong. Effective annual rate $i_a = (1 + r/m)^m - 1$, and continuous compounding $i_a = e^r - 1$.

3. Choosing the discount rate

MARR selection in industry (weighted average cost of capital plus risk premium), OMB Circular A-94 rates for federal analyses, and why a fast-depreciating technology argues for a shorter horizon rather than a higher rate.

Quality management lens

Discounting the cost-of-quality stream. Prevention and appraisal spending occur early; failure costs arrive later and grow with deployment scale. Time value of money is what lets you compare a prevention dollar today with an external-failure dollar in year four. We build the discounted cost-of-quality curve and show why the optimum is rarely zero defects and rarely zero prevention.

Agentic AI corner

Ask your assistant to build a cash-flow diagram and present-worth calculation for a short scenario, then check it against the provided library. Note where it silently assumed a compounding period. Agents are good at scaffolding and bad at units.
