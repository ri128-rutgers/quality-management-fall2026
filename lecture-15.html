Objectives

  • Integrate the semester's methods into a defensible life-cycle cost analysis of an AI-enabled system.
  • Reflect on how the classical engineering economy toolkit does and does not transfer to intelligent systems.

Six things to leave with

  • The method: define the capability, build the CES, estimate by phase-appropriate method, quantify uncertainty, compare alternatives, decide, govern, and improve the model itself.
  • The classical toolkit transfers; what changes is that inference is a marginal cost, models depreciate by drift, data is capital, and agents act.
  • Open problems: model depreciation accounting, data valuation, cost estimating for capabilities that cannot be fully specified.
  • Your final briefing: recommendation first, confidence second, drivers third, in eight minutes.
  • The AI-use appendix is not a confession; it is the verification record a review board will ask for.
  • PDCA on your own cost model: actuals are the measurement system.
