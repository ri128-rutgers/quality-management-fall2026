Final project – Life-cycle cost analysis of an AI-enabled system

30% of the final grade · proposal Oct 21 · interim Nov 25 · final report and briefing Fri Dec 18, 11:59 PM ET

Submit in CanvasAll assignments

The assignment

Select an AI-enabled system and produce a complete, defensible life-cycle cost analysis comparing at least three acquisition or hosting alternatives, with uncertainty quantified and a decision recommended. The project is individual. It is the piece of work from this course you should be able to show a hiring manager or a cost review board.

Acceptable systems include a capability from your own organization (anonymized and with only public or invented figures where necessary), a system from the provided list in Canvas, or a proposed system you define. It must be genuinely AI-enabled: a model that is trained or fine-tuned, hosted, monitored, and retrained, with inference cost that scales with use.

Required content

  1. System and mission definition. What the system does, who uses it, the demand profile over the analysis horizon, and the life-cycle phases.
  2. Alternatives. At least three, drawn from: third-party API, managed cloud with rented accelerators, owned on-premises accelerators, hybrid, and a status-quo or non-AI baseline. At least one alternative must differ in ML technique (for example RAG versus fine-tuning, or a distilled model behind a router) or be agentic (the system acts through tools, not only recommends) rather than differing only in hosting. State what is held constant, including the accuracy or quality floor every alternative must meet.
  3. Cost element structure and ground rules. Three-level CES; analysis horizon; base year; discount rate with justification; treatment of taxes if applicable; escalation assumptions by line item; refresh and retraining cadence; model economic life with evidence.
  4. Estimate. Method for every element (analogy, parametric, machine-learned CER, build-up, expert judgment) with data sources cited; all prices current and sourced with URL and access date.
  5. Uncertainty. Monte Carlo treatment with distributions justified, at least one correlation, S-curve, confidence level of the point estimate, and tornado chart.
  6. Decision analysis. At minimum, sensitivity and breakeven analysis; where the decision is staged, a decision tree or real-option treatment. Multi-attribute comparison if non-cost attributes are decisive.

  7. Retraining and drift. State the retraining policy assumed (fixed cadence or drift-triggered), the evidence for the drift rate, and the cost of monitoring.

  8. Recommendation. Which alternative, at what funding level, with what conditions and what data to collect next.

  9. Energy, assurance, and compliance. Energy consumption and cost of the compute; evaluation, red-teaming, and documentation costs; security, privacy, and AI-governance attestation costs that apply (e.g., under the NIST AI RMF or ISO/IEC 42001).

AI-use appendix

Required, one page: which assistants or agents you used, for which parts, what they produced that was wrong or unsupported, and how you verified what you kept. The analysis, model, and interpretation must be your own. You will be asked to explain your model in a short recorded Q&A if the instructor has questions about authorship.
