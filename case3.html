Case 3 – Own, rent, or call: an AI assistant for a hospital scheduling desk

8% of the final grade · due Wednesday, November 18, 11:59 PM ET · covers Weeks 9–11

Submit in CanvasAll assignments

The situation

A regional health system wants a language-model assistant that drafts responses for its central scheduling desk (about 2.1 million patient messages a year, growing 9% annually), reading the patient's message and the scheduling rules and proposing a reply that a human agent approves or edits. Patient data cannot leave the system's cloud tenancy. Four ways to get it:

  • A. Third-party model through an API inside the tenant, published per-token pricing, prompt-engineered with the rules in the prompt.
  • B. Retrieval-augmented generation over the rules and past approved replies, same hosted model.
  • C. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning of a mid-size open model on rented accelerators in the tenant.
  • D. Distilled small model behind a router that escalates hard messages to the hosted model, plus a semantic cache.

Demand profile, quality floor (agent-approval rate the system will accept), current published prices you must source and cite, staffing, and evaluation costs are in case3_data.xlsx. An optional E. agentic alternative (the assistant also checks availability and proposes slots via tools) is described with its tool-call and retry profile.

What you deliver

A four-page briefing for the health system's CIO and chief quality officer, plus the case3_tco notebook with your cited prices filled in.

  1. The cost stack. For A–D, itemize one-time development, per-message inference, data and evaluation, and people over five years, discounted. Show cost per thousand messages in year 1 and year 5. (25 pts)
  2. The accuracy threshold. At what agent-approval rate does D stop being cheapest? Explain why quality is a constraint here, not an afterthought, using the cost-quality frontier. (15 pts)
  3. Own versus rent. For C, the breakeven utilization at which owning accelerators beats renting; sensitivity to a 20% per-year decline in rental price. (15 pts)
  4. Model economic life and retraining. State the economic life you assumed for the model and the retraining cadence, with the evidence behind it. (10 pts)
  5. Agentic option (E). Add E to the comparison with its token-per-task distribution and human-checkpoint cost. Where does it land, and what would have to be true for it to win? (15 pts)
  6. Data quality and supplier quality. One paragraph each: which data-quality dimension most threatens each alternative, and the supplier-quality terms (SLA, deprecation notice, exit) you would require from the model provider. (10 pts)
  7. AI-use note. (10 pts)

Prices must be current published prices with URL and access date. The model is graded, not the numbers.
