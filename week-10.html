Costing the cloud, data, and machine learning

▶ Interactive lectureOpen notebook in ColabDue Nov 11: Discussion post and one peer replySubmit in Canvas

Learning objectives

  • Build a total cost of ownership model for an ML capability under three hosting patterns: third-party API, managed cloud with rented accelerators, and owned on-premises accelerators.
  • Decompose AI cost into training, fine-tuning, evaluation, inference, data, and people, and express inference cost per unit of work (per request, per thousand tokens, per image).
  • Model accelerator economics: utilization, depreciation horizon, energy and cooling, and the breakeven utilization at which ownership beats rental.
  • Quantify the cost consequences of adaptation and efficiency techniques: prompt engineering, retrieval-augmented generation, parameter-efficient fine-tuning, full fine-tuning, distillation, quantization, model routing, and response caching.

Lecture segments

1. The AI cost stack

A cost element structure for an AI capability. Training compute $= \text{accelerator-hours} \times \text{rate} / \text{utilization}$; inference compute scales with demand and model size; data and evaluation are recurring.

2. Own, rent, or call an API

Breakeven analysis with utilization as the decision variable. Sensitivity to price-per-hour decline (a negative escalation rate), reserved and committed-use discounts, and egress costs. Students source current published prices and cite them; the model, not the numbers, is what is graded.

3. The economics of adaptation techniques

A capability can be obtained by prompting a large model, adding retrieval (RAG), parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA), full fine-tuning, or distilling to a small model. Each shifts cost between one-time development, per-inference compute, data, and evaluation. Quantization and batching reduce inference cost at some accuracy cost; a router that sends easy requests to a small model and hard ones to a large model, plus a semantic cache, changes the marginal cost curve. We build the comparison as an alternatives problem with accuracy as a constraint, not an afterthought.

4. Depreciating a model

Accelerators depreciate by tax schedule; models depreciate by drift and by competitor releases. Setting an economic life for a model and treating retraining as a capital-like refresh or as O&S. Energy cost per inference and its trend.

Quality management lens

Data quality as a cost driver. Data quality dimensions (accuracy, completeness, consistency, timeliness, validity, uniqueness) each carry acquisition and remediation cost, and poor data quality shows up as model failure cost downstream. The cost-quality frontier for a model (accuracy versus cost per inference) is the trade curve a quality manager owns.

Agentic AI corner

Add an agentic alternative to the cost stack: orchestration overhead, tool-call latency, retries, guardrails, and per-task token distributions. Kapoor et al.'s argument that agent benchmarks must report cost alongside accuracy is the economic framing for the week.

Open articles and videos

Free, no login required. Read or watch what the lecture segments point to; the rest is optional depth.

  • video — Andrej Karpathy – [1hr Talk] Intro to Large Language Models. The cleanest general-audience explanation of training, fine-tuning, and scaling; watch before the cost-stack lecture.
  • article — Epoch AI – Trends in the dollar training cost of machine learning systems. Empirical growth rates for training cost; note the uncertainty bounds.
  • article — Sevilla et al. (2022) – Compute Trends Across Three Eras of Machine Learning. Training-compute doubling times.
  • article — Hu et al. (2021) – LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning; read the Abstract and Section 4 for the cost argument.
  • article — Hinton, Vinyals & Dean (2015) – Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network. The origin of distillation as a cost-reduction technique.
  • article — Kaplan et al. (2020) – Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models. Why compute, data, and parameters trade off as they do.
  • reference — Hugging Face – PEFT documentation. Practical reference for parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods.
  • article — Yao et al. (2022) – ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models. The loop that makes an agent cost more than a prompt.
