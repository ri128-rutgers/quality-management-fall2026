Risk, value, and cost in decision making

A COO asked: 'Should we pilot first?' The pilot cost $600k. The right answer was not yes or no; it was 'the pilot is worth $1.1M as information, so yes — and here is what it must measure.' Today: decisions under uncertainty, real options, FMEA, and when to retrain.

Objectives

  • Structure a decision with decision trees and expected monetary value, compute the expected value of perfect and imperfect information, and apply utility for risk-averse decision makers.
  • Value managerial flexibility with real options (defer, expand, abandon, switch) using binomial lattices.
  • Combine cost with non-monetary value using multi-attribute methods and defend weight elicitation.
  • Frame model retraining as a sequential decision under drift and solve a small Markov decision process for the optimal retraining policy.

Six things to leave with

  • Decision trees turn a choice into expected values; the value of information is what a pilot is worth before you run it.
  • Risk aversion changes answers; exponential utility with a stated risk tolerance makes that explicit.
  • Real options value flexibility — the option to abandon after a pilot or expand after adoption — which plain discounting undervalues.
  • FMEA's risk priority number and expected loss usually agree on the top item and disagree on the rest; reconcile them.
  • Retraining under drift is a repeated decision: a threshold policy ('retrain below X') usually beats a calendar.
  • Agents that act create a new FMEA category — unintended actions; a human checkpoint is appraisal cost you can price.
  • Multi-attribute methods handle latency, explainability, and lock-in; elicit weights explicitly.

Segment 1

Decision analysis

Trees, EMV, sensitivity, and EVPI/EVSI applied to a pilot-before-scale decision for an AI deployment. The pilot is an information purchase; price it.

Segment 2

Real options

Discounted cash flow undervalues staged programs. Binomial lattice valuation of the option to abandon after a pilot and the option to expand after adoption exceeds a threshold.

Segment 3

When to retrain: drift as a sequential decision

Performance decays with data drift; retraining costs money and restores it. The retrain/monitor/wait choice repeats every period, which is what a Markov decision process describes. A five-state spreadsheet model is enough to see the answer: the optimal policy is usually a threshold (retrain when performance drops below X), and comparing it with a fixed calendar cadence shows what monitoring is worth.

Segment 4

Value beyond dollars

AHP and SMART for weighting attributes such as latency, explainability, security posture, and vendor lock-in; cost-effectiveness frontiers.

Quality management lens

Risk tools from the quality body of knowledge

Failure mode and effects analysis (FMEA) and its risk priority number are the quality profession's version of expected loss; we reconcile RPN with expected monetary value and show where RPN misleads. Drift monitoring is control-phase work in DMAIC; the retraining MDP prices it.

Agentic AI corner

Agents this week

Agents that act on systems create a new failure mode category for FMEA: unintended actions. Human-in-the-loop checkpoints are appraisal cost; the cost of a wrong autonomous action is external failure cost. Price the checkpoint.
